INDEPENDENT PRODUCT EXPLORATION / 001

Strava:
rethinking value.

An independent product strategy exploration into how a mature consumer fitness platform can strengthen perceived subscription value, pricing architecture and monetisation.

01 / THE BRIEF

What is the opportunity?

Strava operates at the intersection of fitness tracking, social motivation and performance insights. This exploration asks how its subscription proposition can make value clearer to different athlete segments while supporting sustainable business growth.

The goal is not to recommend a price in isolation. It is to understand which value different users recognise, need and are willing to pay for.

02 / PROBLEM SPACE

A value perception challenge.

Subscription products can face a gap between the features a company offers and the outcomes customers believe they receive. A single bundle may not communicate value equally well to casual participants, consistent recreational athletes and performance-focused users.

Working hypothesis — not a finding
Different athlete segments may value different combinations of progress insights, planning tools, safety features, community and motivation. Packaging and messaging could make those differences easier to understand.

This hypothesis will be tested against public product information, competitor propositions and user conversations before any recommendation is made.

03 / RESEARCH PLAN

Evidence before recommendations.

The research will combine three evidence streams:

  • Product and pricing audit: map current plan structure, feature access, trial and upgrade journeys using publicly available information.
  • Competitive review: compare relevant fitness and activity products on target segment, value proposition, packaging and monetisation model.
  • User conversations: speak with a small, varied group of active and occasional fitness-app users to understand behaviours, unmet needs and subscription decisions.

Any small-sample interview insights will be labelled qualitative and directional, not presented as statistically representative.

04 / KEY QUESTIONS

What we need to learn.

  • Which Strava capabilities do users rely on most, and why?
  • What makes a fitness subscription feel worth paying for?
  • Where do free users experience friction or see insufficient incremental value?
  • How do users compare Strava with alternatives they already use?
  • Which benefits resonate with distinct user segments?

05 / NEXT STEPS

From discovery to decisions.

The next iteration will document the current pricing and feature architecture, synthesise competitor patterns, analyse user conversations and define opportunity areas. Recommendations will include rationale, trade-offs, prioritisation, success metrics and testable experiments.

This is an independent portfolio project. It is not affiliated with, commissioned by or endorsed by Strava. No internal Strava data is used.
